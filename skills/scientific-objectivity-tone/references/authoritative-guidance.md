# Authoritative Guidance Map

This file records the main sources used to shape `scientific-objectivity-tone`.
It is not required for routine skill use. Read it when revising the skill or adapting it to a new study type.

## Source-Derived Rules

1. Use study-design-specific reporting guidance.
   - Basis:
     - ICMJE recommends using reporting guidelines such as CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, and STARD.
     - EQUATOR is the main discovery hub for reporting guidelines.
   - Sources:
     - https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/manuscript-preparation/preparing-for-submission.html
     - https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/

2. Plans and protocols must specify outcomes completely.
   - Basis:
     - SPIRIT and CONSORT require explicit outcome definitions, including measurement variable, analysis metric, aggregation, and time point.
     - Prespecified outcomes should stay consistent with protocol and registry, and changes should be reported with reasons.
   - Sources:
     - https://www.consort-spirit.org/item14-outcomes
     - https://spirit-statement.org/spirit-statement/
     - https://www.spirit-statement.org/

3. Interpretations must remain bounded by design, limits, and uncertainty.
   - Basis:
     - ICMJE says to avoid unqualified conclusions not supported by the data and to distinguish clinical from statistical significance.
     - STROBE asks for cautious interpretation, discussion of limitations, bias, imprecision, and generalizability.
   - Sources:
     - https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/manuscript-preparation/preparing-for-submission.html?page=0
     - https://www.strobe-statement.org/download/strobe-checklist-wide-cohort-case-control-and-cross-sectional-studies-combined-pdf

4. Results should not rely on `p` values alone.
   - Basis:
     - ICMJE recommends presenting findings with uncertainty indicators and warns against relying only on hypothesis testing.
   - Source:
     - https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/manuscript-preparation/preparing-for-submission.html

5. Plans should address rigor, bias control, and transparent reporting explicitly.
   - Basis:
     - NIH defines scientific rigor as unbiased and well-controlled design, methodology, analysis, interpretation, and reporting.
     - NINDS guidance emphasizes testable hypotheses, bias control, multiplicity, missing data, negative results, validation, and reproducibility.
   - Sources:
     - https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/reproducibility
     - https://www.ninds.nih.gov/funding/preparing-your-application/preparing-research-plan/rigorous-study-design-and-transparent-reporting

6. Language should state association versus causation explicitly.
   - Basis:
     - NIH public communication guidance explicitly distinguishes association from causation by study design.
   - Source:
     - https://www.nih.gov/about-nih/science-health-public-trust/tools/checklist-communicating-science-health-research-public

7. Reporting should include harms, not only benefits.
   - Basis:
     - CONSORT states that readers need harms as well as benefits to make balanced decisions.
   - Source:
     - https://www.consort-spirit.org/item27-harms

8. Transparency and reproducibility require material, data, code, and protocol availability when possible.
   - Basis:
     - Nature editorial policy requires that others be able to replicate and build upon published claims, with disclosure of restrictions.
   - Source:
     - https://www.nature.com/natprogbrainhealth/editorial-policies/reporting-standards

9. The skill should actively detect and remove spin.
   - Basis:
     - PLOS Biology review defines spin as reporting practices that distort interpretation or overstate results, including inappropriate causal language and emphasis on favorable secondary findings.
   - Source:
     - https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.2002173

10. Systematic reviews and review protocols should align with PRISMA and PRISMA-P.
   - Basis:
     - PRISMA improves complete reporting of why a review was done, what methods were used, and what results were found.
   - Sources:
     - https://www.prisma-statement.org/
     - https://www.prisma-statement.org/protocols
